1. Executive Summary & Problem Space

Modern commercial mobile security software relies almost exclusively on cloud connectivity (REST APIs, signature downloads, and centralized cloud heuristics). However, in high-profile criminal operations—including VIP vehicle ambushes, cash transit vehicle heists, ATM tampering, and digital kidnapping—hostile actors deploy Radio Frequency (RF) Jammers to deliberately eradicate cellular, Wi-Fi, and GPS communications, effectively blinding traditional cloud security platforms.

VEX introduces an autonomous, 100% offline detection engine that operates directly on standard consumer Android hardware without requiring software-defined radio (SDR) accessories. By synthesizing multi-band baseband telemetry, GNSS Carrier-to-Noise density ($C/N_0$), and physical Pedestrian Dead Reckoning (PDR), VEX not only detects intentional RF interference in under 500ms but also estimates the effective blast radius of the jammer and navigates the victim along the optimal physical escape vector to restore connectivity.

2. Offline Detection Physics: Natural Dead Zone vs. Malicious Jammer

A central engineering hurdle is distinguishing benign signal attenuation (e.g., entering an elevator shaft, underground basement, or mountain tunnel) from malicious wideband RF flooding. VEX executes a tripartite differential evaluation algorithm:

📉 Natural Signal Attenuation

Gradual RF Drop: Signal fades progressively over 10 to 30 seconds as physical obstacles attenuate the carrier wave.

Asynchronous Loss: 4G/5G drops first, while Wi-Fi or satellite GPS often retains intermittent connectivity.

Correlated Motion: Accelerometers verify physical displacement (e.g., walking into a parking garage).

🚨 Malicious RF Jammer Flooding

Instantaneous Multi-Band Collapse: Cellular (LTE/5G), Wi-Fi (2.4/5GHz), and GPS plunge simultaneously within <500ms.

High RSSI with Collapsed SINR: Antenna detects extreme RF raw energy (jamming carrier), but Signal-to-Interference Ratio drops below -12 dB.

Stationary Anomaly: Complete communications collapse occurs while the device IMU registers zero physical movement.

3. The 3 Offline Technical Detection Vectors

Vector 1: Multi-Interface Baseband Collapse (TelephonyManager): VEX registers low-level telephony listeners (`TelephonyCallback.CellInfoListener`). If all serving and neighboring cell towers transition from active LTE/5G state to `NO_SERVICE` concurrently with Wi-Fi disconnect, a high-priority RF anomaly flag is raised.

Vector 2: GNSS Satellite $C/N_0$ Drown (GnssStatus): Under clear atmospheric conditions, standard smartphones track 15–30 satellites with Carrier-to-Noise density ($C/N_0$) values between 28 and 45 dB-Hz. A GPS jammer saturates the 1575.42 MHz band with high-power Gaussian noise. When VEX detects >80% of active satellites plunging below 12 dB-Hz simultaneously, RF jamming is verified.

Vector 3: Baseband Power Ramping Metric: Under jamming conditions, the cellular modem initiates aggressive "power ramping" (transmitting at maximum +23 dBm attempting to pierce the noise floor), inducing rapid battery voltage drops and thermal baseband spikes without active application traffic.

4. Mathematical Modeling: Jammer Radius ($R$) Estimation

RF transmission adheres to the Friis transmission formula and the Log-Distance Path Loss Model. The received jamming power $P_{j,r}$ at distance $d$ is expressed as:

P_j(d) = P_t - 10 \cdot n \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{d}{d_0}\right) - X_\sigma \quad \text{where } n \approx 3.2 \text{ (Urban Environment)}

A mobile device experiences total jamming when the Jammer-to-Signal Ratio exceeds the receiver's threshold ($J/S \ge \gamma_{th}$). By categorizing the spectrum absorption profile across frequency bands, VEX profiles the hardware class of the attacking transmitter:

5. Pedestrian Dead Reckoning (PDR): Navigating Without GPS

Because satellite signals are neutralized, VEX activates the internal hardware Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU):

  • Step Counter (`TYPE_STEP_DETECTOR`): Computes cumulative distance traversed ($D = \text{Steps} \times 0.75\text{m}$).
  • Magnetometer & Gyroscope (`TYPE_ROTATION_VECTOR`): Tracks azimuth heading ($0^\circ - 360^\circ$) relative to magnetic North.
  • Last Known Base Station Vector: Evaluates the physical direction of the primary cell tower recorded prior to blackout.
